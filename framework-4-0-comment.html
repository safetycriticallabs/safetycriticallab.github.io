Safety Critical Labs/ Framework/ 4.0 public comment

Version 4.0 is open for comment.

The AI Requirements Framework is being revised from version 3.7 to version 4.0, the first major revision since the framework was published. Before 4.0 is deposited as the standard SCL assesses against, the full draft is open to anyone who wants to read it and say where it is wrong. This page holds the draft, what changed and why, what to read if you have an hour rather than a week, how to send a comment, and what SCL does with every comment it receives.

Where this stands today. Version 3.7 remains the published standard and the version SCL assesses against; it is unchanged by this draft and stays citable at its own DOI. The 4.0 draft is not a published standard, carries no DOI, and binds nothing. It is deposited only after this window closes and every comment has been dispositioned, under the rule in SCL's framework development methodology that a version with normative changes is not published without an external technical read and a public comment window.
01

What 4.0 changes, in plain terms

Version 3.7 asked whether a requirement was met. Version 4.0 asks what the evidence can actually establish, and makes the answer visible on the certificate. Every verification now names the class of evidence it produces: deterministic evidence closes a requirement; statistical evidence supports it at a stated confidence and never closes it; bounded evidence holds at a declared budget and nowhere else; surveillance evidence is an obligation in service. A verification can now resolve to insufficient evidence as well as pass, fail or not applicable. At the Safety-Critical tier an insufficient-evidence outcome blocks certification; below it, the outcome is carried on the certificate as a declared residual with closure due at the first surveillance. The residual list replaces the risk score on the certificate face.

The Safety-Critical tier now attests an architecture, not only a set of rates. A new requirement set, AI-14 Runtime Assurance, makes a conventionally verifiable monitor and an assured fallback the condition of a Safety-Critical determination. A learned component for which no such monitor and fallback can be built cannot be certified Safety-Critical under this framework, and the text says so.

Every rate, score or coverage figure must now be measured on a declared evaluation population with its strata stated (new Section 1.7), and every threshold must carry a derivation basis against criteria the standard itself states (Section 1.5). SCL sets no numeric thresholds, and the illustrative values in Section 5 are labelled as unsourced. The monitoring, alerting, response and logging cycle that several requirement sets repeated is stated once (new Section 4.6). The information classification controls move from AI-1 into a conditional set, AI-15. AI-2 is rescoped to disparity across declared strata; AI-5 applies where a system produces generative or free-form output. Ten 3.7 identifiers are retired and nine requirements are new; AI-1, AI-2, AI-5 and AI-7 are renumbered to close the gaps, each renumbered heading carries its 3.7 number for this edition, and the identifier map at the end of the draft's Document Revision History maps every 3.7 identifier to 4.0.

The standard is now two parts. Part 1 (SCL-ARF-001) is the standard itself: the requirements, the verification statements, the verification matrix and the conformity basis. Part 2 (SCL-ARF-002), issued with it at the same version, carries what supports applying the standard and nothing that defines a requirement: the AI classification determination, the verification evidence templates and the full revision record. Part 1 cites Part 2 as a normative reference for the classification determination and the required content of the evidence records, and for nothing else.

02

What to read if you have an hour

The design decisions live in about twenty pages of Part 1: Section 1.0 (how the framework is developed and governed), Sections 1.5 to 1.7 (threshold derivation and adequacy criteria, the conformity basis, declared evaluation populations), Section 4.1 (evidence classes, verification outcomes, the verification statement template, the residual list), Section 4.6 (the monitoring cycle), and AI-14 (Section 3.4). If you have standing in one domain, the Related Domain Standards crosswalk under each requirement is where a wrong citation would be caught; it carries no conformity burden, but it should still be right.

The questions SCL most wants answered: does the conformity model hold up; is runtime assurance the right condition for the Safety-Critical tier; is there a requirement that cannot be verified as written, or a verification whose success criterion does not discriminate; and is there a sentence that would be read differently from the way it was meant.

03

How to comment

Download the template, or use the same three fields in an email: the clause or identifier the comment is about, the comment itself with its reasoning, and proposed replacement text where you have it. A comment with proposed text is far more likely to change the document than one without, because it can be adopted as written. Send it to kevin.williams@safetycriticallabs.com with "SCL 4.0 comment" in the subject line, by December 6, 2026. Say whether you are commenting for yourself or for an organisation, and whether SCL may name you in the published disposition record; unnamed is the default.

Comments are accepted only by email. Replies on social channels are read but are not comments, because a comment has to be a dated record that receives a written disposition, and a thread cannot be that.

04

What happens to every comment

Each comment receives one of four dispositions: accepted; accepted with modification; rejected, with the reason; or out of scope, with where it was routed. Every commenter receives a written reply with the disposition. A comment that changes normative wording goes through the same change control as any other edit: approved by the SCL Principal, built from the source, validated. When 4.0 is deposited, the disposition record is published on this page beside the final version, so that anyone can see what was raised and what was done with it.

05

How the draft was made

The draft was built from version 3.7 under SCL's documented framework development methodology, from a structured review of the 3.7 text conducted in October 2026. Drafting, build execution and validation were assisted by AI drafting tools operated under Safety Critical Labs' direction; every normative sentence was read and approved by the SCL Principal before the draft was released for comment, and no sentence was adopted without that reading. No external technical committee governs the text; this window, and the external readers invited before it opened, are the review the text receives. The readers are named in the published record at deposit, with their consent.
